# Which polynomials in u and ū carry a configuration in their hull
- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); mirrored on Mathlib v4.35.0
- **Date** — 2026-10-18

Near a point u of a circle, with uū algebraic, Roy's strong six
exponentials theorem can exclude a number z from ℒ̃ at a candidate when
the hull span(1, u, ū, z, z̄) carries a 2×3 configuration. A research
note from a cloud session (its Theorem E) said exactly which
polynomials z in u and ū have that property. Its proof was condensed.
This round (R18) had it refereed twice, then formalised it in 25 nodes.

## What was proved

Write s = |u| and v = u/s, so that a polynomial in u and ū with
algebraic coefficients is a Laurent polynomial in v.

- **Theorem E.** The hull W carries a configuration exactly when it is
  five-dimensional and in one of three classes: (P) W contains
  αu² + βu³ ≠ 0; (Q) W = span(1, u, ū, v̄² + ev², βv̄³ + ζv² + ev³)
  with |e| = |β| = 1 and ζ = −βe²ζ̄; (R) W = span(1, u, ū, g, ḡ) with
  g = G(v) + κ·conj(vG(v)), |κ| = 1, G(0) = 0, deg G ≥ 2. The classes
  are disjoint, and each forces dimension 5.
- **Corollary E.** With an exponent of absolute value at least 4 only
  (R) remains. For z ≡ Aū³ + Bū² + Cu² + Du³, with N = |A|² − |D|² and
  Δ = AC̄ − BD̄, a configuration exists exactly when
  NΔ(|Δ|² − |u|²N²) = 0.
- **At a candidate**, under Roy's theorem as a hypothesis, no member
  of the three classes lies in ℒ̃.

## How

- **Refereeing first.** Two referee agents checked the note's proof
  without seeing each other's work. Both found it correct, with six
  gaps of exposition, and filled them. Each also wrote a separate
  decision procedure in exact arithmetic: 762 and 773 test spaces, with
  no disagreement. Both scored Theorem E the same under the blueprint's
  rubric, third among the results not found and not routine.
- **The formal route is shorter than the note's.** Multiplied by a
  power of v, a configuration factors through a coprime pencil
  (a₀, a₁) of polynomials of degree at most 2. There are four such
  pencils up to change of basis. One count of degrees rules out
  (1, X² + αX) and (X + β, X² + γX). The pencil (1, X) gives class (R)
  through the real pencil normal form, and (X, X² + β) gives (Q).
  Dimension 4 or less is excluded by Theorems B and C.
- Seven proof agents, then one more pass. Two proofs went over the
  size rule, because both had to prove that each class forces
  dimension 5. That fact became its own node, the twenty-fifth. The 25
  files have 4,228 lines of content, the largest 275.
- **One rejection.** Three proofs came back FAILED in seconds. The
  platform's soundness guard rejects any `macro`, and an agent had
  written a small tactic macro for membership goals. Inlined, the
  proofs were accepted. Neither a local compile nor the statement
  check catches this.
- **A credit found on the way.** The configuration of class (Q) is the
  one in the proof of Diaz's Théorème 2 of 2004, whose hypothesis
  allows any rotated axis. An earlier check had called the rotated axis
  the project's own.

## What is not proved

- **Only polynomials in u and ū.** The note's next step, rational
  functions of u with poles off 0 and ∞, is not attempted.
- **The rest of the note's Theorem D** (Baker, Gel'fond–Schneider, six
  and four exponentials at a generic point of a circle) is scoped at
  about 14 nodes and not formalised.
- **"Not routine" is an editorial judgement**, made by two agent
  referees under a rubric fixed in advance. No person has refereed it.
- **No step towards Diaz's conjecture.** The results say what Roy's
  theorem can exclude through these hulls, and the statement at a
  candidate is vacuous if the conjecture holds.

## What remains open

- Theorem E for all of Q̄(u): poles off {0, ∞} come in conjugate pairs,
  and the note expects one sumset condition per place.
- The rest of Theorem D.
