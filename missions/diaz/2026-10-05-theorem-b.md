# Which four-dimensional extensions carry a 2×3 configuration
- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); mirrored on Mathlib v4.34.0
- **Date** — 2026-10-05

On 4 October, Theorem A showed that near a point of a circle no rank-one 2×3 configuration exists on generic
data. Theorem B, from the same research notes, settles the next case: one extra number. It was proved on paper
on 4 October and formalised today.

## What was proved

- **The classification.** Let u ∉ Q̄ with uū algebraic, put H₀ = Q̄ + Q̄u + Q̄ū, and take z ∉ H₀. Then H₀ + Q̄z
  carries Q̄-independent x₁, x₂ and y₁, y₂, y₃ with all six products x_i y_j in it exactly when z ∈ H₀ + Q̄w for
  w = u², w = ū² or w = 1/(u − a), with a algebraic and non-zero.
  - With Roy's strong six exponentials theorem, the three cases are Diaz's exclusions at a candidate (2007,
    Cor. 5(1) and 5(4)).
  - The statement does not involve e^u, so it keeps its content if Diaz's conjecture holds.
- **Four steps, each a node:**
  - a configuration in a space of dimension at most four forces a geometric progression b, bh, bh², bh³;
  - cubics with P₁P₂ = P₀² have the normal form g·(Q₀Q₁, Q₁², Q₀²);
  - a progression through 1, u and ū contains u², ū² or some 1/(u − a);
  - the three spaces carry explicit configurations.

## How

- **A configuration is a progression.** With h = x₂/x₁, the space B = x₁(Q̄y₁ + Q̄y₂ + Q̄y₃) and its image hB fit
  in a space of dimension four, so they share a plane. Inside B that plane meets its own image under h⁻¹, which
  gives b with b, hb and h²b in B. The space is then b(Q̄ + Q̄h + Q̄h² + Q̄h³).
- **u is a Möbius function of h.** 1, u and 1/u are b times cubics P₀, P₁, P₂ in h with P₁P₂ = P₀². Unique
  factorisation gives P₀ = gQ₀Q₁, P₁ = gQ₁² and P₂ = gQ₀², with Q₀, Q₁ linear and g in their span.
- **Reading off w.** The two coefficients of g decide the case: u², ū², or 1/(u − a).
- The notes' first step used Theorem A when z is transcendental over Q̄(u). It turned out to be unnecessary: any
  h outside Q̄ is transcendental, so the argument works for every z.
