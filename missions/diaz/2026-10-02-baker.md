# Baker's theorem, by Schneider–Lang in several variables
- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); checked on Mathlib v4.34.0 against the mirror
- **Date** — 2026-10-02

Baker's theorem in its qualitative form says that if logarithms of
algebraic numbers are linearly independent over ℚ, then together with 1
they are linearly independent over the algebraic numbers. It was an Open
node on the board, `Schanuel.baker_linear_forms_in_logarithms`, and
several of the mission's results carried it as a hypothesis. It is now
proved, along the route Bertrand and Masser found in 1980 and Waldschmidt
wrote out in Chapter 4 of Diophantine Approximation on Linear Algebraic
Groups. A survey of other provers on 1 October found no machine-checked
proof of qualitative Baker, of Schneider–Lang in several variables, or of
the Cartesian Schwarz lemma.

## What was proved

Twenty-five results. At the top, `DiazModulus.baker_two_logs` states
Baker's theorem for two logarithms in exactly the form the mission's nodes
assumed, so it can be passed to them. Below it, the board's node itself;
then Theorem 4.5 of the book, Baker's theorem for a basis of a number
field through the trace form; then the criterion of Schneider–Lang for
ℂ^{d₀} × (ℂ^×)^{d₁} with d₀ ≤ 1 (the book's Corollary 4.2); and the
analysis under it.

## How

The criterion is proved directly, as in §4.6 of the book. Siegel's lemma
gives an auxiliary function, an integer combination of exponential
monomials whose derivatives of low order are small on a lattice grid.
Those derivatives are algebraic numbers of bounded house and denominator,
so Liouville's inequality makes them vanish. Take the first order at which
some derivative at a grid point does not vanish; it exists because a non-
zero combination of the monomials has a non-zero derivative at the origin.
A Schwarz lemma for Cartesian products bounds that derivative from above,
Liouville's inequality bounds it from below, and the parameters are chosen
so that the two bounds conflict. No zero estimate is needed.

The Schwarz lemma for products is the hardest piece, and it is proved here
with only one-variable analysis. Each step writes the function as its
Hermite interpolant in one coordinate plus a multiple of the node
polynomial, and bounds both on a larger disc; the steps telescope over the
coordinates. Mixed derivatives are written as lists of directions and
matched with Mathlib's `iteratedFDeriv` on coordinate vectors. One gap in
the book needed repair: in step 5 of §4.6, vanishing to a given order in
each coordinate does not survive the change of variables that the proof
makes, while vanishing to a given total order does. With total order, only
the constants change.

Baker's theorem then follows as in the book, through the trace form: the
embedding matrix of a basis of the number field is invertible because the
discriminant is non-zero (`Algebra.discr_not_zero_of_basis`). The proofs
total 3,320 lines; the largest is 261, so every one is under the mission's
300-line rule.

## What is not proved

- Only the qualitative theorem. There is no lower bound for a linear form
  in logarithms: no Baker–Wüstholz, no Matveev, no effective constant.
- Schneider–Lang is proved for d₀ ≤ 1, which is what Baker's theorem
  needs, not in the book's general form.
- The nodes that carried Baker as a hypothesis still state it. Their
  unconditional forms are a separate batch.
- The gap in step 5 of §4.6 was found and repaired here. It was not
  compared with any published erratum.

The 24 new statements went up at 10:03, and `baker_two_logs` and the
board's Baker node became milestones 96 and 97. The proofs were sent in
ten waves, top first, so that each node had joined the mission through an
importer before its own proof was checked. Every wave came back as
expected, with the leaves accepted and the rest as sketches. By 12:46 all
25 nodes were Proved and all 25 were members of the mission.

## What remains open

Effective forms of Baker's theorem, and with them any quantitative use.
Roy's strong six exponentials theorem is still carried as a hypothesis by
the mission's nodes that need it; its proof would need a multiplicity
estimate. The mission's two open leaves, the real half of (S) and (NT),
are unchanged.
