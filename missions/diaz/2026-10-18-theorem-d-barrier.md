# What the classical theorems cannot refute about Diaz's conjecture
- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); mirrored on Mathlib v4.35.0
- **Date** — 2026-10-18

Diaz's conjecture says no non-zero logarithm of an algebraic number has
algebraic modulus. Many classical theorems are statements about the set
ℒ of such logarithms. A cloud session's research note (its Theorem D)
showed that a larger set satisfies all of them and does contain a
number of algebraic modulus. So those theorems, used as properties of
ℒ, cannot prove the conjecture. This round (R19) formalises it in 16
nodes; one part, under Roy's theorem, was already formal from R16.

## What was proved

Let u be a point of a circle, with uū algebraic, that is transcendental
over the field generated by ℒ̃ = Q̄ + Q̄ℒ, and put ℒ_u = ℒ + ℚu + ℚū.

- **Theorem D.** ℒ_u contains ℒ and u, is stable under conjugation,
  and satisfies with ℒ replaced by ℒ_u: Baker's theorem,
  Hermite–Lindemann, Gel'fond–Schneider, the six exponentials theorem
  over ℚ and the four exponentials theorem in transcendence degree one.
  Roy's theorem for ℒ̃ implies it for Q̄ + Q̄ℒ_u.
- **The barrier.** Such points exist on every circle, because the field
  generated by ℒ̃ is countable. So some ℚ-space containing ℒ has all
  these properties and a non-zero element of algebraic modulus.
- **ℒ itself** has the same properties, and the conjecture says exactly
  that ℒ has no non-zero element of algebraic modulus.

## How

- **One field fact carries most of it.** If u is transcendental over F
  and uū lies in F, then a + bu + cū is transcendental over F whenever
  (b, c) ≠ (0, 0). An element of ℒ_u is c + pu + qū with c ∈ ℒ and p, q
  rational, so a relation that holds in F forces p = q = 0.
- **Baker for ℒ_u** follows at once, and gives Hermite–Lindemann and
  Gel'fond–Schneider. **Six exponentials** needs neither Roy's theorem
  nor Gel'fond–Schneider, though the note uses both: Baker for ℒ_u
  makes the two families independent over Q̄, the inversion lemma of
  R16 puts the six products in ℒ̃ ∩ ℒ_u = ℒ, and the theorem for ℒ
  applies.
- **Four exponentials** goes through a trichotomy: the ℚ-span of the
  entries lies in ℒ, on a line, or in ℚu + ℚū. The third case uses an
  evaluation at 0 in place of the note's irreducibility argument.
- A scoping study first estimated 14 nodes and 1,100 to 2,100 lines.
  The round took 16 nodes and 1,423 lines, from 53 to 135 per file, each
  compiling in under 16 seconds of wall time. Six proof agents; no
  rejection this time.

## What is not proved

- **The model has no exponential function.** It concerns membership in
  ℒ only, not the values e^z; the theorems are taken as properties of
  the set, not of exp. A stronger barrier would need a model of
  exponentiation, in the manner of Kirby's or Zilber's exponential
  fields, which this is not.
- **Roy's clause is relative**: it says Roy's theorem for ℒ̃ implies it
  for the larger set, not that it holds outright.
- **All 16 results are routine**, and each clause restates a classical
  theorem with ℒ replaced by ℒ_u. The barrier was not found in the
  sources read.
- **No step towards Diaz's conjecture.** The result says which methods
  cannot settle it.

## What remains open

- Barriers that include the exponential function, or other theorems
  (Schanuel-type statements) that ℒ_u may fail.
