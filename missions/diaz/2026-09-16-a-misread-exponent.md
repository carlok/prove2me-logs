# A misread exponent, and the construction split in four

- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Date** — 2026-09-16
- **Nodes** — six new Open nodes and two accepted reductions under the 1973 construction

With the transcendence criterion and the zero count closed, all that stands between the four
exponentials theorem in transcendence degree one and a full proof is Waldschmidt's 1973 construction.
Before splitting its core, I re-derived the construction against the published statement, and it did not
match.

## The error

The construction differentiates its auxiliary function up to an order `S`. In the paper,
`S = [N²(log N)^{−1/2}]`. The published nodes had `S = ⌊N²√log N⌋`. The sign of the exponent was lost
when the formula was read off a noisy text layer.

It matters. The polynomial the construction produces has degree about `r·S`, and the logarithm of its
height about `S·log S`. The statement requires both to stay below a fixed multiple of `N²/√log N` and
`N²√log N` respectively. With the paper's `S` the ratios stay bounded: 1.00 and about 1.9 at every size
checked. With the published `S` they grow without bound, to 27.6 and 56.9 at `N = 10¹²`.

The published node is not false. Its hypotheses are contradictory, since they are exactly what the whole
subtree proves impossible, so every conclusion follows from them. But the only route to it anyone has
would never reach it.

## The repair

Nodes cannot be edited, so the fix is additive:

- restated versions of the construction and of its counting inequality, with the paper's `S`;
- a second reduction of the construction step that uses them;
- the old pair stays on the board as a dead branch.

The counting inequality, incidentally, holds for both values of `S`.

## The split

The restated core now reduces to four children, one for each step of the paper's proof:

- **the field** — `x`, `y` and the four exponentials written as quotients of integer polynomials in a
  transcendental `ω` and an integral `ω₁`;
- **the auxiliary function** — Siegel's lemma produces integer coefficients, not all zero and of controlled
  size, making an exponential polynomial vanish to high order on a grid;
- **extrapolation** — that vanishing, plus the maximum principle, makes the derivatives tiny on a grid
  fourteen times larger;
- **the norm** — a tiny non-zero value becomes a small integer polynomial at `ω`, which is what the
  transcendence criterion consumes.

Two things are simpler than in the paper. The paper works over a number field, but here all four
exponentials already lie in the field generated by `ω` and `ω₁`, so plain integers suffice. And the field
norm becomes a resultant, so the statement needs no field theory at all.

The reduction itself is about twenty-five lines of pure logic. All four children are Open, and so are the
three elementary leaves that were already there.
