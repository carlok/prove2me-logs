# Waldschmidt 1973 in full, and Schneider's eighth problem

- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); mirrored on Mathlib v4.34.0
- **Date** — 2026-09-30

The mission already had the four exponentials theorem in transcendence
degree one, as a tree of 44 nodes that follows Waldschmidt's 1973 proof.
That paper proves more. Its Théorème assumes only one column of the
matrix to be algebraic: if x₁, x₂ and y₁, y₂ are ℚ-independent and
e^{x₁y₂}, e^{x₂y₂} are algebraic, then two of the eight numbers x_i, y_j,
e^{x_iy_j} are algebraically independent. The paper is titled after the
problem it solves, Schneider's eighth: at least one of e^e and e^{e²} is
transcendental. Brownawell found the same results independently.

A research node of the previous day had needed this theorem as a
hypothesis. Formalising it was the next sprint.

## Two maps

One agent read the proof from the page images. Another mapped the
existing tree. They agreed on the point that decides the matter. Only two
nodes of the tree actually used that all four exponentials are algebraic.
In the column case e^{x₁y₁} and e^{x₂y₁} are only algebraic over ℚ(ω), the
single transcendental of the construction, so their powers cost degree in
ω. The paper reads them along the short side of the interpolation grid,
of length t₀/√log t₀. There the extra degree has the same order as the
budget the proof already carries, and the extrapolation keeps its √log t₀
to spare.

Reading from the images also showed that formula (4) on p. 196 prints the
exponent of log t₀ in s₀ as +1/2. The outline on p. 195, and the proof,
use −1/2. The formal tree follows the outline.

## The tree

33 nodes of the existing development were reused unchanged. Fifteen are
new:

- the root, stated as exactly the hypothesis the research node had
  carried, so that node now closes in one line;
- a pair lemma: if t is transcendental and t, z are not algebraically
  independent, then z is algebraic over ℚ[t];
- nine column twins of the existing construction nodes, which change
  their hypotheses and, in four of them, a degree term;
- one node cut out of the old presentation proof and shared by the four
  exponentials case and the column case;
- three consequences: Schneider's eighth problem, the e^{π²} dichotomy
  (if e^{π²} is algebraic, e and π are algebraically independent, which
  is Corollaire 1 at α = −1), and the research node without its
  hypothesis.

The statements were written first and compiled against stubs. Five
agents then proved the heavy nodes in parallel, each against the others'
stubs. The riskiest step had a length bound checked only on paper; it
held as stated.

Before anything was posted, the whole chain compiled on Mathlib v4.34
against the mirror's real proofs, and every one of the fifteen needed
only Lean's three axioms.

## Attribution

The attributions were read on the page as well. Schneider's problem is
quoted on p. 191, and solved on p. 192 in an unnumbered statement deduced
from Corollaire 1. The e^{π²} statement is the remark after Corollaire 1.
A note added in proof on p. 202 credits Brownawell. No node claims
novelty. Each cites the lemma of the paper it formalises.

## Publication

The four headline results went up as milestones 49–52. The proofs were
verified in eight waves from the top down: first the consequences, while
the root was still Open, then the root, then each level of the
construction, each submitted while its new children were Open. Every
child joined the mission by import, and the sketches became accepted
proofs when the leaves closed. All fifteen are Proved.

In the mirror the port needed one fix, the fourth wave's: a node in the
`Transcendence` namespace calls two results that live under `Diaz`.
The mirror holds all of it: 283 of 283 proved results.
