# The third size wave: one block copied twice becomes eight nodes

- **Mission** — Diaz's modulus conjecture, `3045e100-83a7-4863-9c02-21f90105f182`
- **Environment** — `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1); mirrored on Mathlib v4.34.0
- **Date** — 2026-09-25

Two proofs in the four exponentials subtree formalise Lemmas 4 and 7 of
Waldschmidt's *Solution du huitième problème de Schneider* (J. Number Theory 5,
1973): `FourExp.aux_linear_system` (1,420 lines) and
`FourExp.norm_to_polynomial_alg` (2,648). Each carried the same 1,046-line
block of helpers, byte for byte. Neither imported anything.

## The map

Two agents read the files before anything was proved. One split the shared
block; the other traced what the second proof actually used (290 of its lines
were never reached) and isolated its core. Both second proofs were then written
against stubs of the proposed nodes and compiled, which tested every statement
against its consumers. Carlo approved the map.

Three things changed from the earlier sketch:
- **One proposed node is Mathlib.** The derivative formula for exponential
  polynomials, 134 hand-written lines in the block, is Mathlib's Leibniz rule
  for iterated derivatives plus 25 lines.
- **A glue node was added.** Written against the general nodes, both consumers
  needed the same 103 lines to reach their reduced linear system. Our own size
  rule says a shared 103 lines is a node, not two copies.
- **The length lemma became two.** Stated as one node with a finite-sum half
  and a product half, its sum half quantified over a type inside a
  conjunction, and every use of the product half then left a universe
  unsolved. Two lemmas in Mathlib's style fixed that.

## The nodes

| node | lines | what it says |
|---|---|---|
| `Transcendence.length_sum_le` | 52 | the length of a finite sum of integer polynomials is at most the sum of the lengths |
| `Transcendence.length_mul_le` | 87 | the length of a product is at most the product of the lengths |
| `Transcendence.exists_int_pow_repr` | 110 | scaled powers of an algebraic number are integer combinations of a fixed basis, with coefficients at most `Aⁿ` |
| `Transcendence.exists_modByMonic_length_le` | 281 | reduction modulo any polynomial multiplies the length by at most `Cⁿ` |
| `FourExp.exists_pow_mul_pow_le_exp_sq_mul_sqrt_log` | 125 | the one asymptotic estimate at the scale `N²/√log N` |
| `FourExp.exists_iteratedDeriv_presentation` | 561 | the derivatives of an exponential polynomial at a grid point, presented through the four exponentials, for any coefficients |
| `FourExp.exists_iteratedDeriv_reduced_presentation` | 171 | the same, with the unknowns and the reduction modulo `Q` |
| `FourExp.exists_int_norm` | 328 | the core of Lemma 7: the norm from `ℚ(ω, ω₁)` down to `ℚ(ω)`, with size control |

The length of a polynomial here is the sum of the absolute values of its
integer coefficients; Mathlib has no such notion, and the old block carried its
own calculus for it in several places.

## On the board and in the mirror

The eight statements published within a minute. The proofs went in four waves:
1. the five nodes that import nothing new;
2. the reduction bound and the presentation;
3. the glue node;
4. the two second proofs.

All ten were ACCEPTED at the first attempt, with explanations attached and read
back. On the board, each new node's graph lists every proof that imports it:
sixteen edges.

`carlok/diaz-modulus-lean` `09c2ded` counts 254 of 254.

Porting found a bug in the porter itself:
- **The bug.** When a local helper's name collides with a declaration elsewhere
  in the library, the porter renames the helper. The rule also caught
  *qualified* uses. `norm_to_polynomial_alg` has a local helper named
  `length_sum_le`, and it also calls the new node `Transcendence.length_sum_le`;
  the rename broke that call.
- **The fix.** Now only unqualified uses, and uses qualified by the file's own
  namespaces, are renamed.
- **One side effect.** A comment in `circle_points_indistinguishable` had been
  pointed at a renamed helper instead of the platform node it names, and now
  reads correctly.

Two stale v4.34 patches, written for the old versions of the two proofs, went
with them. The new proofs build on v4.34 unchanged.
