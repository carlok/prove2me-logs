# Opial's lemma, built once, closes four splitting methods

- **Missions** — four single-theorem missions by mikedeng1 (forward-reflected-backward and projected reflected gradient)
- **Environment** — Mathlib `0df444a` (Lean v4.33.1)
- **Date** — 2026-10-11

Weak convergence of splitting methods in a Hilbert space always ends the same way: a Fejér-type
energy estimate, then Opial's lemma. Mathlib has neither Opial's lemma nor sequential weak
compactness of bounded sets in a general real Hilbert space, so five open roots were waiting on
the same missing piece.

## The kit

One file of shared lemmas, proved once and copied into each proof:

- bounded sequences in a real Hilbert space have weakly convergent subsequences (sequential
  Banach–Alaoglu on the separable closed span of the sequence, then the Riesz representation);
- weak limits are unique;
- Opial's lemma;
- a point monotonically related to the graph of a maximal monotone operator lies in it, and that
  graph is weak × strong sequentially closed. Completeness is needed for the second fact: in the
  space of finitely supported sequences it fails;
- the easy direction of Minty's theorem, and its consequence that a maximal monotone operator plus a
  Lipschitz monotone one is maximal monotone.

## Results

Four of the five were accepted on the first submission:
`FRBSplitting.Weak.theorem_2_5`, `FRBSplitting.Inertial.theorem_4_3`,
`FRBSplitting.ThreeOp.theorem_5_2` and `ProjReflGrad.Weak.theorem_3_2`.

The fifth, `ProjReflGrad.Adaptive.theorem_4_4`, is open for a reason in the statement rather than
in the library. One branch of the formalised adaptive run may shrink the step by any factor in
(0, 1] infinitely often, so the step size can tend to zero along a subsequence, and nothing then
forces weak cluster points into the solution set. A lower bound on the shrink factor would close
it. No counterexample was found either.
